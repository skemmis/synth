Mycel

Cribbed from Physarum slime mould simulations. Your organism is a few thousand blind specks. Each one sniffs three points ahead, turns toward the strongest trail and leaves a little trail of its own. That is all, and it is enough to grow veins. A speck that reaches a pellet is fed and may bud a new one. A speck that goes too long without food dies, so veins to nowhere wither. A thin trickle of new specks buds from the heart on the left. You cannot steer. You can only lay lure and salt.

What to try
  • Do nothing for 30 seconds. The colony booms on the two near pellets, drains them and crashes back to the trickle that buds from its heart on the left. That is the problem to solve.
  • Draw a lure line from a busy vein out to a far pellet. Once a few specks get there and feed, the vein holds itself and you can stop.
  • Salt cuts. Slice a vein that leads to an emptied pellet and the specks in it go back to work elsewhere.
  • Watch the average. Left alone it sits near 1,200 specks. A good shepherd holds over 2,000. Pellets run dry and new ones land at random.
  • Change how long a speck lives. Short lives make tight veins that cannot reach far. Long lives make a fog.
