Synth Menagerie

Eight small playable toys, each lifting one mechanism from an existing simulation and asking what a cell game built on it would feel like. They share nothing but the amber pellet. The point is to find out which of these your hands like, so the notes say what to do in the first minute.

Gulpbe the cell

Cribbed from pressure soft bodies and the Spore cell stage. A ring of beads, springs and one pressure number.

Your hands do steer with the pointer, squeeze through gaps, spit mass to shrink and kick.

Try first push into a gap narrower than you. Then eat five pellets and try the same gap.

Playtest The size trade-off is real with no rule written for it: a starting cell passes a 36 gap but not 26, a well-fed one needs 50. Closest of the eight to the membrane-and-cilia cell.

Wireheadsensing and neurology

Cribbed from Braitenberg's vehicles, with breeding borrowed from The Bibites.

Your hands do connect two antennae to two cilia with plus and minus wires. Twelve wires in all.

Try first cross the pellet wires and watch it turn toward food. Then press Breed.

Playtest Hand wiring scores about 27 to 38 in the test dish. Breeding reaches about 80 by finding a lopsided idle that sweeps in spirals, which is not something a person draws first. That gap is the fun.

Sproutgrow, don't build

Cribbed from Cell Lab and Karl Sims' unfolding body programs.

Your hands do fill in four cell-type cards: job, when to split, what the halves become.

Try first run Tadpole at 3x, then change one dropdown and drop a fresh germ.

Playtest Most genomes die, a few take over the dish. Tadpole filled the dish in 6 of 6 runs. Two tails on one mouth pull against each other and starve, which nobody programmed.

Cinderpitmadcap chemistry

Cribbed from falling-sand toys and Noita.

Your hands do pour seven materials with a brush. Fewest grains wins.

Try first flood an open cup so the pellets float out, then lay a moss fuse to the bangdust.

Playtest Fuses, floats and bombs all work from two or three rules per grain. An unplanned one: flooding the oil cup lifts the oil out over the rim, and the pellets follow. Moss overgrows anything you flood.

Mycelbody as a crowd

Cribbed from Physarum slime mould simulations.

Your hands do lay lure and salt. You cannot steer anything directly.

Try first wait for the first crash, then draw a lure line from a live vein to a far pellet.

Playtest Left alone it booms and busts around 1,200 specks on average. A simple shepherding script holds about 2,100. Boom and bust is strong and may want softening.

Bloomfieldbody as a pattern

Cribbed from Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion.

Your hands do paint feed, walls and spores into a living stain.

Try first draw one thin feed line to the nearest pellet and watch spots divide along it.

Playtest On quiet ground spots hold still forever. A feed line makes them bud along it one after another. It is slow and meditative, the least game-like of the set.

Gaitkeeperyou build, evolution drives

Cribbed from Sodarace and Karl Sims' evolved creatures.

Your hands do place joints, bones and muscles. Breeding picks the muscle rhythm.

Try first press Let it go, then Breed a gait, on the same body.

Playtest Untrained bodies move about 15. Bred ones reach 400 to 500 in half a second of breeding. Small body edits change the gait completely.

Motleydesign the physics

Cribbed from Particle Life.

Your hands do edit a five by five table of likes and dislikes, live.

Try first start from Blank, then give one colour +1 toward pale.

Playtest Random tables crack between 0 and 176 pellets in 25 seconds, so the table matters a lot. When every colour could smell pellets the game was trivial, so now only pale can.

Tests proposed next, for a yes or no

  1. Wired cell. Gulp's soft body with Wirehead's wiring. Sensors and cilia sit on the membrane and you connect them, so steering comes from the body instead of the pointer. 22 beads, 4 sensor sites, 4 cilia sites, wires at -1/0/+1, 40 s rounds, breed 15 generations of 20
  2. Cell in the pit. Gulp's cell swimming inside Cinderpit's grain world, where water, oil, moss and bangdust are both hazard and tool. 180 x 112 grains, 20 bead cell, 6 chambers, cell can carry and drop one material
  3. Grown machines. Sprout's cards driving the parts sandbox from the other thread, so slingshots and whips are grown from a germ instead of placed by hand. 4 types x 6 settings, bond kinds rod/spring/rope, dish cap 160
  4. Bigger brains. Wirehead with two inner neurons, a touch sense and a hunger sense, to see where hand wiring stops being readable. 8 senses, 2 inner, 2 cilia, breed 30 generations of 40, 3 dishes
  5. Living pond. Wirehead swimmers that breed in the dish as they eat, with you as gardener moving pellets and poison. start 40 swimmers, cap 120, mutation 0.2 per wire, split at energy 1.5
  6. Species hunt. A search over Motley tables for bodies that travel, heal and feed, to give a shelf of found species to start from. 2,000 tables, 5 colours, half-step values, 35 s each, about 25 minutes on four threads

Not built yet

Lenia and neural cellular automata (smooth creatures that regrow when cut) and Avida (programs that evolve to compute) were on the list. Both are harder to put hands on, so they come after a first reaction to these eight.
